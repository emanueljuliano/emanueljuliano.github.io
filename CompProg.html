Competitive Programming

At the beginning of my undergraduate studies, I became very interested in competitive programming. The most famous competition is the ICPC, which consists of several phases until the World Finals. In each phase a team of three members must solve as many problems as possible from a list of around 12 problems whithin 5 hours.

Here are some precious memories from my years as a contestant and some of my favorite problems.

Favorite Problems

Here are some of my favorite competitive programming problems. Problems enter this list either because they made me excited about learning something new, or because they are related to my favorite topics in CP:

  • Codeforces 628A - EhAb AnD gCd, I like giving this problem to beginners!
  • SEERC 2019 - Game on a Tree, what happens if the game is played on a directed graph? The answer is surprising.
  • CSES - Eulerian Subgraphs, the solution with Burnside's lemma is my personal favorite.
  • Codeforces 428E - Mother of Dragons, you can actually prove Turán's theorem from it.
  • IOI 2007 - Pairs, I was shocked when I discovered this.
  • 2024 ICPC Gran Premio de Mexico - Highest Score APPQ, such a weird way of thinking about nimbers.
  • CodeChef - Faulty System, or an improved version: Given a graph, find two disjoint spanning trees.
  • Codeforces 441F - Royal Questions, thanks Kaio for solving this one with me and remembering it.
  • Deltix Round, Spring 2021 - Love-Hate, this idea is so cool, and it was so rewarding to solve it in contest time.
  • Brazilian IOI Qualifiers 2023 - Shuffled Cube, I was a teacher and judge at the Brazilian IOI qualifiers for three years, and I created this problem!

Extra, not CP-related: I really like the book Combinatorial Problems and Exercises by László Lovász, and problems 3, 4, and 5 of the Automorphisms of Graphs section kind of blew my mind.
